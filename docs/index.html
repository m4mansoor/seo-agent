Getting a backlink hasn't changed in fifteen years

Find a site that gives links. Create an account. Find the field, or the editor. Place the link. Check that it actually went live, and that it's a real link, and that it's followed. Then do it again. Fifty links is fifty of those, and most of the time goes on the parts that have nothing to do with judgement.

So people pay to skip it. And what they pay for is where it goes wrong.

What actually goes wrong

  • You pay an agency $150 to $400 a link and never see the method or the site list. When they leave, the knowledge leaves with them.
  • You buy a list of "1,000 sites" and it's full of ghosts like the one above. Authority scores lag reality by years.
  • The link goes up as plain text, or the host adds nofollow after you post. It looks fine on the page. Nobody checked.
  • Fifty links land in one afternoon, all reading the same phrase. That's the clearest signal links were bought — and you paid for it.

What the agent does instead

Twenty-six tools, grouped by the four things you actually care about. The tool reference lists every one.

It plans with you, and says why

Most tools take an order. This one reads your situation first and argues for a pace your site can carry.

Tells you what you need before you start
Ask what it takes and you get a number: which sites are open right now, how many more open with a mailbox, which need you at the screen.
Proposes the campaign shape with a reason on every line
Pace from how many sites already link to you. Anchor mix tightened when the profile is thin. A followed share that looks natural. Ask for more than is wise and it says so once, then builds what you asked. It's your site.
Reads your page for keywords and anchors
Keywords from the target page itself, spread across the five anchor types so your profile isn't one phrase forty times.

It builds the link the way that site actually works

Not "go to the site and sign up". The exact method, and it learns when the guide is wrong.

Knows the method for every site
The playbook for that kind of link, the steps with the exact button and field names, and what the site requires.
Builds in a real browser
Login-free sites build in seconds with a proof screenshot. Account sites are built by the assistant in its own browser — signing up once, signing in ever after.
Never forgets what a site taught it
Which field gives the followed link, a length cap, a password rule the page never shows. Found once, recorded, and the next build starts from the truth.
Stops at gates instead of faking its way through
A captcha, an email code or a social login is a gate. It says what the site wants in plain words and offers the ways through.

It checks before it counts

A link only exists if the public page shows a real hyperlink to you, with the anchor you agreed, and passes authority.

Verifies every link on the live page
Three checks: is your URL a real hyperlink, does the anchor match, is it followed. Plain-text URLs and links the host quietly made nofollow don't count.
Reports what it built, from its own record
Every link with its anchor, where it lives, the site's authority and when. Not a backlink index that won't show a new link for weeks.
Keeps the proof
A screenshot of every placement, a live URL, a status you can trust. A link it couldn't verify is reported as unverified, never counted.

It keeps watching, and keeps the pace

Links drop. Profiles get deleted. And a burst of links is the pattern that gets a site looked at.

Paces everything
Links arrive steadily across days and methods — at most 5 a day and 20 a week to any one site. Many sites under one subscription.
Watches the links afterwards
Weekly re-checks of everything it placed, every referring domain to your site, what's new and lost each month, spam flags, and a disavow file.
Finds what competitors have and you don't
Point it at two or three competitors and it lists the referring domains they have that you lack, with the method to build each one attached.
